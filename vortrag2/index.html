Where the test comes from

The three part test we used is a compression, built for one session, and the name is mine. The requirements underneath it are not mine. They come from two widely used frameworks, and both list seven, not three. Worth knowing the day you have to argue about a model in writing.

EU, Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI (2019)

  1. Human agency and oversight
  2. Technical robustness and safety
  3. Privacy and data governance
  4. Transparency
  5. Diversity, non-discrimination and fairness
  6. Societal and environmental well-being
  7. Accountability

NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0 (2023), characteristics of trustworthy AI

  1. Valid and reliable
  2. Safe
  3. Secure and resilient
  4. Accountable and transparent
  5. Explainable and interpretable
  6. Privacy-enhanced
  7. Fair, with harmful bias managed

What the highlighting means

Traceability is not a requirement of its own in either list. The EU guidelines name it as the mechanism that serves transparency. In research data management it stands on its own, as principle R1.2 of the FAIR principles: data are associated with detailed provenance.

Accountability is a requirement in both lists, and the EU guidelines name auditability as its mechanism. In banking supervision the same idea appears as documentation an unfamiliar party can follow, and validation independent of whoever built the model.

Reproducibility appears in neither list. It is the norm of empirical and computational science, carried over here to model output. In AI regulation it is the least codified of the three, and that gap is itself worth remembering.
